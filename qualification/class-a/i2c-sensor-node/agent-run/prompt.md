I2C Sensor Node PCB — Make It Release at Four Layers, Fix the Review Items, Compact (Synth MCP only, NO fill; case 1 lessons applied)

1. State of the board

Your workspace holds the seed board: i2c_sensor_node.synth, its placement sidecar, README.md and user-registry/ (parts the board uses, including polyfuse_500ma). It is an 87-part two-layer STM32G030F6P6 node with a BME680, a 24LC256 EEPROM, a CH340C USB-UART bridge, a USB-C receptacle, a 5 V JST input, an SWD header, a GPIO header and I2C headers. A first agent run could not release it: with the current Synth build the seed fails with 3 open nets (+3V3, I2C_SCL, LED_STATUS), 3 vias in pads and about 90 signal vias without a ground-return via, and the router reports that two parts cannot be escaped on two layers: the USB-C receptacle J2 (0.5 mm pitch, pads touching) and the STM32 U2 (TSSOP-20, 0.65 mm pitch); the router's own remediation is "an inner plane at 4 layers". The seed export is about 83.0 x 47.0 mm with a courtyard fill of about 34.8 %.

The router is chaotic: a small layout change can break a net or put a via in a pad, so work in small steps and keep only steps that release. The only tools are the Synth MCP and Read, Write and Edit. Do not invent tool results, part numbers or datasheet values; a value you are not certain of is written "needs sourcing".

2. Goals, in order

G0. Already done by the orchestrator (disclosed in the README under "Edits applied by the orchestrator"): the seed is already four layers with a JLCPCB 1.6 mm stackup and 0.6 mm / 0.3 mm vias, because the two-layer version cannot escape the USB-C receptacle and the TSSOP-20 (97 blockers). Do not change the layer count or stackup. Keep every function and part; replace a part only if the router still reports it unescapable on four layers (then one of the same function, and report it). Inner layers are the ground plane and a 3.3 V/5 V power plane as Synth assigns them.
G1. Make the board release (release_ready, route_complete, 0 DRC errors, no via in a pad, no pour island, no blocking finding): follow the placement review's advice actions (synth_place_with_hints, run_routing=false) and the export findings, move parts by role, pin coordinates in the sidecar, re-route, and export into a fresh folder each time. Never pass allow_incomplete=true. Never edit exported KiCad files. Do not add hard `near:` or `edge:` hints to many parts at once: an earlier run did and the auto-placer scattered the parts (outline 93 x 91 mm); change one small group of parts per step and keep the step only if it releases.
G2. Compact: start from the release and make the board smaller in steps, keeping a step only if it releases with every G4 gate; aim for an outline no larger than 83.0 x 47.0 mm; if the board only releases larger, a released board up to 15 % larger in area is acceptable and you report the size. External connectors (USB-C, JST) flush with their board edge within 0.3 mm (pin headers may sit inboard); four mounting holes at the corners, clear of parts and copper. You may call synth_compact_layout only after the board releases with zero ground-return-via blockers.
G3. No fill: add no parts. This board is released and compacted, not filled; the part set stays as it is except for the replacements the review items below require.
G4. Meet this review checklist (a reviewer found these on this board and on the irrigation board; avoid every one, and fix rather than justify):
 - Zero ground-return-via blockers is a gate at every step: every signal via has a ground via within about 1.53 mm. A step that adds one is a failing step. If a net (USB, I2C, a zener line) forces such a via, change the placement or the part, not the claim.
 - Every external line of J3 and the other connectors is protected: a 3.3 V zener (registry d_zener_3v3) behind a series resistor on 3.3 V signals, never a 5 V clamp on a 3.3 V line; write the current arithmetic in the design notes.
 - Netclasses are declared AND assigned to nets; exported track and via sizes match the declared ones, or the declaration is revised.
 - The AMS1117 dissipation at the stated load (about 0.51 W at 300 mA from 5 V to 3.3 V) is checked against its thermal limit from the datasheet, or the load is lowered or the regulator replaced.
 - A plain default export is valid with no optional flag and no split ground pour; three exports into fresh folders are byte-identical apart from random ids.
 - Per-pin decoupling: each decoupling capacitor within 4 mm of the supply pin it serves (pad to pin). No through-hole pad within 2.1 mm of an SMD pad. Silkscreen is left to Synth's post-routing label pass.
 - Input protection, if the board has a supply input: TVS or clamp standoff at or above the maximum input, placed before any series MOSFET or regulator, worst-case clamp below the regulator's and MOSFET's ratings, numbers from datasheets.
 - No clamp or TVS whose worst-case clamp exceeds the absolute maximum of what it protects: the 5 V PRTR5V0U2X diodes D8 and D9 sit on the 3.3 V SWD lines (behind 100 ohm series resistors) and exceed the STM32 input maximum; replace them with the 3.3 V zener (registry d_zener_3v3, cathode on the signal, anode on ground) and write the current calculation. The USB data and CC clamps stay.
 - Every fine-pitch part has an escape the router actually produced.
 - Buck/regulator and decoupling at the pins they serve; current assumptions written; declared net classes assigned to nets.
 - Distances: SWD header within 15 mm of the processor, reset button within 8 mm of the reset pin, decoupling at its pins, test points beside their circuit.
 - Every part in a named functional group so the schematic has no OTHER region, and the groups stay few (about 12 or fewer) so the schematic stays on one page.
 - Orderable BOM: manufacturer, MPN, package and rating from the registry; "needs sourcing" rather than a guessed number.
 - Case 1 lessons (the irrigation controller, 2026-10-23 to 2026-10-31); every one was a reviewer finding there, so meet it from the first export:
   * KiCad ERC on the exported schematic reports 0 errors, and KiCad DRC reports 0 errors, 0 unconnected and 0 silk_overlap; Synth's post-routing label pass moves or hides a reference that would overlap silkscreen, so do not fight it.
   * Decouplers at their pins means within 4 mm of the supply pin it serves, measured both pad to pin and part centre to pin (the placement review uses the centre). Put the capacitor's supply pad toward the pin (rotate it 180 degrees if needed). If one cannot sit that close, say which, and name the datasheet sentence that asks for no distance; never write "the router could not".
   * The exporter re-legalises parts after you place them, so measure through-hole to SMD pad gaps on the EXPORTED board and keep every one at 2.4 mm or more (JLCPCB's Danger limit is 2.1 mm). A step that leaves one under 2.4 mm is a failing step.
   * Replace a part only when a review item forces it; add nothing (no fill step, no extra capacitors, resistors, LEDs or test points).
   * Every value you state in the design notes (LED current from its forward voltage, bleeders, crystal load, zener current, AMS1117 dissipation) comes from the selected part's registry or datasheet figures with the arithmetic written; a figure you do not have is "needs sourcing", not an assumption.
   * A capacitor derates with DC bias; where the registry has no bias figure say so and give the arithmetic at 100 %, 50 % and 30 % of its value.
   * Three exports of the same final source and sidecar into three fresh folders must be identical once the router's random ids are masked; report their hashes.
   * Hidden labels are fine; hidden or moved parts are not: confirm the component count and every connection are unchanged after each step.

PCB layout quality rules (mandatory, applies to every board)

Keep the full functionality, the component set, the connectors and the mounting holes. Add no decorative text: no board name, no revision text, no functional headings, no logos. Silkscreen carries only what identifies parts, connectors and test points. Four rules, in this order: compactness (no unnecessary gaps between related parts), grouping (the parts of one circuit sit together), consistency (repeated parts, connectors and identical circuits are aligned and oriented alike), functionality (never trade electrical performance, manufacturability or serviceability for looks). Every gap that remains must have an electrical, mechanical or manufacturing reason.

Work by role, not by name. Decide each circuit's role from its connectivity, then place by these rules:
1. Support parts hug the part they serve. A decoupling capacitor sits within a few millimetres of the supply pin it decouples, a crystal and its load capacitors beside the clock pins, a reset or boot network beside the pin it drives, pull-ups and dividers beside the node they set, a flyback or clamp diode beside the switch it protects. Do not leave a support part standing alone in open board area. After every placement, read the placement review: when it says a support part is too far from a pin and names a hint such as `near: U2.vcc`, write exactly that hard `near` hint for that part in the source and place again, and repeat until the warnings clear or you can say why one cannot. The review lists the worst eight; after you fix them, place again to see the next ones.
2. The power entry owns a corner or an end. The input connector, its protection, the reverse-polarity part and the regulator form one contiguous block; each regulator has its inductor and its input and output capacitors touching it, laid out as its guidance asks, with a tight switching loop. The block must not crowd the processor or the sensitive inputs.
3. The main processor sits near the middle of the board unless routing says otherwise, with its decoupling in a tight ring and its programming or debug header, reset button and boot parts close to it and reachable. Memory and other parts wired mainly to it sit directly adjacent.
4. A peripheral circuit sits beside the connector it serves. When a driver, interface IC or protection network is wired mainly to one connector, place the whole circuit directly beside that connector, on that connector's edge, with its resistors and capacitors around it.
5. Identical circuits are placed alike. When two or more circuits use the same part types and the same connectivity (several output drivers, several identical sensor interfaces), give them the same internal layout, the same orientation, and the same pitch along the edge, so the copies read as copies. Align their connectors on one baseline with even spacing. After every placement, read the placement review: when it says a part of one copy is laid out differently from the same part in the first copy and gives coordinates, pin that part at exactly those coordinates (and rotation) in your placement sidecar and place again, until the copies match or you can say why they cannot.
6. External connectors sit flush on the board edge, the edge their mating face points to, with even spacing between connectors on the same edge. Pin headers may sit a few millimetres inboard. Leave plug and cable clearance.
7. Test points sit with the circuit they measure, within a few millimetres of a part on their net; test points for one interface (debug, programming) form one aligned row by that interface. Keep them probe-accessible and out of the processor's escape area. Ground test points are easy to reach.
8. Orientation and alignment. Put like parts (resistors, capacitors, diodes) on shared rows or columns inside a block, give equivalent transistors matching orientation, align IC edges and connector centres on common horizontal and vertical lines, and keep pin-1 marks identifiable. Do not rotate arbitrarily and do not force one orientation where it worsens the electrical layout.
9. Indicators. Status LEDs with their resistors form one row at an edge, near the part whose state they show.
10. Silkscreen. Each reference designator sits beside its part, in the same orientation and size for the same kind of part, clear of pads, copper and other bodies. Where Synth cannot place text, say so in the report instead of working around it.
11. Routing. Route after placement is final: short and direct, 45-degree bends, widths for the current, return paths preserved, noisy and sensitive nets apart, little congestion near the processor, continuous ground pour, no isolated copper islands.
12. Outline and holes. Keep the mounting holes with clearance and no part under the hardware. Keep part and copper setbacks from the edge. Shrink the outline only when the placement truly allows it.
13. Validation and report. Run ERC and DRC, confirm every schematic connection and the component count are unchanged, review the board in 2D and 3D, and report measured numbers: each decoupling capacitor's distance to its pin, each connector's gap to the board edge, each test point's distance to its circuit, and for every group of identical circuits whether their layouts match. Say plainly what you could not do.

14. Release exports only. A qualification board is a release export. Never set allow_incomplete=true on synth_export or any other tool: it switches off the release gate (footprint guarantee, routing and signal-integrity checks) and labels the board a draft. If the export is blocked, read the finding and fix its cause with the Synth tools (for a part whose footprint is not in the KiCad library, use synth_resolve_footprints and name one KiCad really ships; for a routing finding, change the placement or the part). Only if you truly cannot clear a blocker, stop and report that blocker exactly, instead of exporting a draft.

15. Compact after the layout is final. When your placement review has no remaining warnings you can fix and a release export has passed, call synth_compact_layout with file_path set to your .synth file, layout_file_path set to your placement sidecar and out_dir set to a folder named compact in your workspace. Each call makes one attempt and returns its attempts so far; call it again with the same arguments until it returns done true. It scales your layout inward, exports every attempt through the release gates, and keeps the tightest layout that still routes with clean DRC; your original sidecar is kept beside it as .pre-compact. Then export once more from the installed sidecar into your export folder, read the result, and report the final outline size and the attempts list (scale, size, passed or failed and why). Never edit the sidecar by hand to undo or tune the result.

## Design change for this run: no USB-C at all (Jeff, 2026-11-01)
Ten hours of exports and searches showed that every blocker comes from the USB-C port and its data pair (USB_DP / USB_DN: three forced layer changes each at J2's 0.5 mm pad row, D5 and U4). Jeff decided to drop USB-C from the board. The board is powered from the J1 JST XH 5 V input only and is programmed over the SWD header; the J3 UART breakout stays for a serial console through an external USB-UART adapter.
Do this first, as one edit of the .synth source, then export:
1. Remove the USB-C receptacle J2 and everything that exists only for it: the CC pull-down resistors, the VBUS fuse, Schottky and TVS that protect the USB-C input, the CH340C bridge with its capacitors and resistors, the USB data TVS array, and every net that only they used (USB_DP, USB_DN, USB_CC1, USB_CC2, VBUS, the bridge's UART nets). Name each removed part and the reason in REPORT_PASS.md. Remove nothing else and add no part except what this change needs.
2. Keep the J1 input path (5 V in, polyfuse, Schottky, TVS, regulator) and its protection exactly as they are. Keep the MCU UART pins wired to the J3 UART breakout through the existing series resistors and the 3.3 V zener clamps, and keep the SWD header.
3. Add a comment block at the top of the .synth that says: powered from J1 only; programmed and debugged over the SWD header (J-Link/ST-LINK, 3.3 V logic, NRST on the header); serial console on the J3 UART pins with an external USB-UART adapter; the board has no USB.
4. The seed .synth already carries the earlier review fixes (3.3 V zener clamps on J3, the C4/R6 moves). The seed placement sidecar is a near fixed point of the plain export of the old design; delete the removed parts' entries. The plain `export-kicad` re-packs any sidecar that is not already its most compact candidate, so after your first export take positions from the exported board (synth_describe_placement) before you edit the sidecar again.
Then continue with G1-G4 of this brief. Report the release flags of every export in REPORT_PASS.md. No fill.
